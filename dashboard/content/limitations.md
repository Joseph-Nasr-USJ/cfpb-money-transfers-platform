## Limitations

Read every chart with these limits in mind.

1. **Not a representative sample.** The CFPB states that the database is not a statistical sample of consumers' experiences. Complaints come from people who chose to complain, so the results describe complaints in this database, not all consumers, customers or the whole market. We do not generalise from them.
2. **No denominators for companies.** The data has no customer numbers, transaction volumes or market shares. A company with more complaints may simply have more customers or a different product mix. Company figures are shares of complaints, never a quality ranking.
3. **A complaint is not proof of fraud or harm.** The issue is chosen by the consumer, including *Fraud or scam*. The data has no confirmed fraud labels, transactions or balances.
4. **Not a resolution time.** Processing lag is the time from CFPB receipt to the day the CFPB sent the complaint to the company. The *timely response* flag is a yes or no from the company response; neither measures how long a problem took to solve or whether the consumer was satisfied.
5. **Recent months are provisional.** A complaint is published only after the company responds or after 15 days, and some records change later. The latest weeks are incomplete, and the current month and year are labelled as partial.
6. **Category changes.** Product labels changed in 2017. The platform maps the older labels into the current domain and keeps the original label next to the standard one. Comparisons across that date need care.
7. **Geography is at state level.** The data has no street address. ZIP codes are partly suppressed or masked by the CFPB and are not used. Records without a state are shown as *Unknown*. State population is not the number of users.
8. **No individual records and no complaint text.** The dashboard shows aggregates only. The CFPB removed the complaint narratives from the database in September 2026, so the platform does no text analysis.
9. **Description, not explanation.** The analysis is descriptive and diagnostic. A pattern shows where to look next; it does not show a cause. The two machine-learning results describe past data and do not forecast.
10. **Small groups.** Where a group has few complaints, its percentage can change a lot from one complaint. Sparse periods are grouped at a higher level.
